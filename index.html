About

Who's keeping this archive.

I'm a Mathematics graduate from Lancaster University, where I focused primarily on real and complex analysis, measure theory, and functional analysis. More recently, I've gotten curious about taking these concepts into tech, programming, and quantitative finance. This blog is mainly to keep track of what I'm reading and thinking about, but also to share thoughts and ideas. On the rare occasions I'm away from my whiteboard or the terminal, I like to read, and explore new cafes and restaurants.

Topics

Some interesting topics I found and try to look into.

Asset Pricing Theory

  • How Lebesgue measure underlies pricing of complex derivatives and risk assessment, via the Radon-Nikodym theorem.
  • Girsanov's theorem: what happens to dynamics when the underlying probability measure is changed.
  • A Python Monte Carlo simulation showing how a discontinuous step payoff converges numerically to its Lebesgue expected value.
  • How to prevent infinite-money loopholes in continuous-time market models — reading the intro and NFLVR condition of the Delbaen & Schachermayer paper (linked under Resources > Finance).

Stochastic Processes

  • Markov chains, random walks, and martingales as a unified toolkit.
  • The Black-Scholes model and Brownian motion as its driving noise.

Graph Theory & Applications

  • K-planar graphs and generalisations of crossing number.
  • Fraud ring detection and its relation to graph matchings.
