Books

A few books that have shaped my interests.

  • A Mathematician's Apology — G. H. Hardy. A must-read for anyone interested in pure mathematics. Although some of the ideas are outdated (e.g. how number theory has no applications outside academia), it perfectly captured how I felt about doing mathematics.
  • The Selfish Gene — Richard Dawkins. This book changed how I think about optimisation outside mathematics.
  • Fermat's Last Theorem — Simon Singh. Along with Hardy's A Mathematician's Apology, this was one of the books that got me properly interested in number theory.
  • How to Solve It — George Pólya. An actual practical guide to thinking through mathematical problems and understanding how mathematicians approach them.
  • Alex's Adventures in Numberland — Alex Bellos. A good choice for anyone who thinks mathematics is only about memorising formulas.
  • The Code Book — Simon Singh. This was my first introduction to cryptography, and I was immediately fascinated by it.
  • QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter — Richard Feynman. A great example of explaining difficult ideas clearly.
