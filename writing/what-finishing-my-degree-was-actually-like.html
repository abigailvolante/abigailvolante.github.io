What Finishing My Degree Was Actually Like

I have been meaning to share my experience during my final year as an undergraduate for quite some time, but I held myself back for fear of coming across as too honest. At the same time, I do not want to create the impression that things are always easy for me, or to be remembered only for what I have achieved. That sounds rather sad to me, because it erases all the difficulties one has to overcome to get there and reduces everything to a piece of paper, a degree, a result. I hope that my character and my ability to persevere matter just as much as what I have achieved. For this reason, I want to share the messier, more human side of being a student, too.

Mathematics is something I care deeply about, but that doesn't mean academic achievements aren't important to me. I recognise that grades have practical importance, particularly when it comes to future opportunities. I suppose I've always found myself somewhere between wanting to learn for the sake of learning and wanting to do well. I hope there's nothing wrong with that.

Looking back, my final year was not quite the year I had imagined. There were things happening in my personal life, periods of illness, and more than a few moments when keeping up with my studies felt difficult. Yet, somehow, I made it to the end, achieving the highest result I could have hoped for.

A month before my final examinations, I remember sitting in the library, working through problem sets and surrounded by other students. I had eight three-hour examinations ahead of me, all taking place within the space of three weeks. Because I had missed a number of lectures due to illness earlier in the year, there were modules I had barely begun studying. Alongside this, I was navigating a difficult period in my personal life, including the end of a relationship that had meant a great deal to me. Trying to come to terms with that loss while managing the demands of my final year was considerably harder than I had anticipated. Even when I sat down to study, concentrating wasn't always easy. There were moments when waves of sadness would get in the way, and I found myself having to pull my attention back to the work in front of me.

My family and friends were a great source of support throughout this period. My parents would visit every weekend, often bringing groceries, flowers and packed Filipino food. We would go to the same Chinese restaurant each time, a small routine that gave me something familiar to look forward to in an otherwise difficult few weeks.

As the examinations went by, I gradually found my rhythm. In the first week of June, I sat my final paper and went straight home afterwards to take what was probably the most restful nap I had had in months. I remember feeling an enormous sense of relief. For the first time in a long while, there was nothing immediately waiting for me to study.

When my results eventually arrived, I found myself becoming emotional. I had never quite understood why people cried out of happiness or relief, but in that moment, I finally did. Seeing that I had achieved a First-Class degree meant more to me than I had anticipated, particularly given everything I had been dealing with at the time.

I sometimes think back to that afternoon in the library. At the time, everything felt so heavy. I only knew that I had to keep going, to keep showing up every day and doing what I had always done.

If there's one thing I'm grateful for from this experience, it's what I learnt about myself: that I can trust in my own resilience. I didn't feel certain at the time, but knowing that I did it once gives me some confidence that I can do something like this again.
